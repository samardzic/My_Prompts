# Task: Create a Comprehensive, Condensed Summary of the book

## Source Material

The source document is the book **Atomic Habits by James Clear**.

The original book contains detailed explanations, recurring examples, repeated arguments, and concepts that are revisited across multiple chapters.

The objective is to transform the book into a comprehensive, well-organized reference document that preserves its intellectual substance while eliminating unnecessary repetition and excessive detail.

The output should function as a condensed standalone reference, allowing readers to understand the book's essential ideas, frameworks, mechanisms, and practical applications without having to read the original book.

<br/><br/>






## 1. Primary Objective

Create a comprehensive summary of the book in a single Markdown document, targeting approximately **20–30 pages of written content**.

Prioritize:
- Conceptual completeness.
- High information density.
- Clear explanations.
- Logical organization.
- Practical applicability.
- Faithfulness to the author's original ideas.

**Important principle: Condense the content, not the knowledge.**

Do not turn the book into a superficial overview or a collection of short bullet points. Preserve enough explanation for readers to understand not only what the principles are, but also why they work and how to apply them.

<br/><br/>






## 2. Content Analysis and Extraction

Before writing the summary, analyze the entire source document.

Identify and extract:
- Fundamental principles and central arguments.
- Important concepts and terminology.
- Psychological and behavioral mechanisms.
- Frameworks, models, and methodologies.
- Relationships between different concepts.
- Supporting research and scientific explanations discussed in the book.
- Practical examples and case studies.
- Actionable techniques and recommendations.
- Important exceptions, limitations, and qualifications.
- Connections between different chapters.

Do not start writing the summary based only on the introduction, table of contents, or selected chapters.

Process the entire book to ensure that important concepts introduced later are not overlooked.

<br/>


### 2.1 Preserve Conceptual Depth

For each major concept, preserve three elements whenever applicable:

1. **What:** Explain the principle or concept.
2. **Why:** Explain the reasoning, psychological mechanism, or evidence behind it.
3. **How:** Explain how to implement it in practice.

Avoid reducing complex concepts to one-line definitions.

When the author introduces a framework, explain its individual components, how they interact, and when it is useful.

<br/>


### 2.2 Preserve Important Distinctions

Pay particular attention to concepts that may appear similar but have different meanings or applications.

Do not merge concepts simply because they discuss the same general subject.

For example, distinguish between:
- Goals and systems.
- Outcomes and processes.
- Motivation and environment.
- Habit formation and habit maintenance.
- Starting a habit and making it sustainable.
- Immediate rewards and delayed rewards.

Preserve such distinctions whenever they are relevant to understanding the author's arguments.

<br/><br/>






## 3. Eliminate Redundancy and Unnecessary Detail

Aggressively reduce:

- Repeated explanations of previously established principles.
- Repetitive arguments and conclusions.
- Multiple examples illustrating exactly the same concept.
- Excessive storytelling that does not introduce additional knowledge.
- Unnecessary historical background.
- Repeated chapter introductions and transitions.
- Redundant motivational passages.
- Unnecessary descriptions of events or individuals.

When several examples illustrate the same concept, retain the most representative example.

If an example contains a particularly useful practical lesson, preserve that lesson even if the original story is shortened.

Consolidate explanations distributed across multiple chapters into a single comprehensive section where appropriate.

Avoid repeating the same explanation in multiple sections merely to preserve the original chapter structure.

<br/><br/>






## 4. Preserve Practical Knowledge and Implementation Techniques

The practical value of the book is a major priority.

Preserve the techniques, recommendations, exercises, and implementation strategies that readers can actually use.

Give particular attention to:

- Step-by-step procedures.
- Habit-building techniques.
- Behavioral modification strategies.
- Environmental design.
- Planning and implementation methods.
- Methods for tracking progress.
- Techniques for overcoming obstacles.
- Methods for recovering from missed habits.
- Strategies for maintaining consistency.
- Common mistakes and recommended corrections.

Where the source describes a technique, explain:

1. Its purpose.
2. How it works.
3. How to implement it.
4. A practical example, where useful.
5. Potential mistakes or limitations.

Do not remove actionable details in the process of shortening explanations.

<br/><br/>

## 9. Markdown Formatting Requirements

Use the accompanying **`markdown_rules.md`** file as the authoritative reference for processing and formatting Markdown documents.

Maintain a consistent hierarchy. Do not skip heading levels.

<br/>


### 9.2 Header Spacing

Apply the following additional spacing conventions consistently.

**H2 headings (`##`):**

Insert two HTML line breaks before every H2 heading.

Required format:
```

<br/><br/>

```

**H3 headings (`###`):**

Insert one HTML line break before every H3 heading.

Required format:

```

<br/>



### H3 Heading
```

Do not insert these spacers before the first H1 heading.

Do not add unnecessary consecutive spacers.

<br/>


### 9.3 General Markdown Formatting

- Use bullet points for related information.

- Use tables when they improve clarity or make comparisons easier.
- Use bold formatting to emphasize important concepts and terminology.

- Use descriptive headings that communicate the actual content of each section.

<br/>


### 9.4 Technical Markdown Validation

# EPUB and E-Reader Compatibility Requirements

The generated Markdown document (`.md`) will subsequently be converted into an EPUB ebook for reading on e-readers (Kindle, Kobo, PocketBook, Apple Books, etc.).

Therefore, the Markdown document must be designed with **EPUB conversion and e-reader readability as primary formatting considerations from the beginning**.

Do not optimize the document exclusively for desktop viewing, wide screens, or printed pages.

The goal is to produce clean, structured, reflowable Markdown that can be converted into a professional-looking EPUB without requiring substantial manual corrections.


## 1. General Formatting Principles

Use simple, semantic Markdown formatting that can be reliably translated into EPUB.

Preserve:
- Document title and subtitle.
- Chapter and section hierarchy.
- Headings and subheadings.
- Paragraph structure.
- Numbered and bulleted lists.
- Emphasis (bold and italic).
- Blockquotes.
- Tables, where appropriate.
- Images, diagrams, and captions.
- Horizontal separators when they improve readability.

Requirements:

- Use proper Markdown heading hierarchy (`#`, `##`, `###`, `####`).
- Keep paragraphs reasonably short and readable.
- Avoid excessively long paragraphs that make reading on small screens difficult.
- Avoid manual line breaks within ordinary paragraphs.
- Do not use multiple spaces or manual indentation for visual alignment.
- Do not use layout techniques that depend on fixed screen widths.
- Avoid unnecessary HTML and CSS that may not survive EPUB conversion.
- Prefer semantic formatting over visual formatting.

The document must remain readable even when displayed on a narrow e-reader screen.


<br/><br/>






### 2. Tables and Tabular Data

Tables require special attention because traditional Markdown tables often become difficult to read on narrow e-reader screens.

Do not create excessively wide tables.

Requirements:

- Keep tables as narrow and simple as possible.
- Prefer two-column tables where practical.
- Avoid tables with many columns.
- Avoid long paragraphs inside individual table cells.
- Avoid nested tables.
- Do not use tables merely to arrange ordinary text or create page layouts.
- Keep column headings concise.
- Ensure that table content remains understandable when converted to a reflowable EPUB.

**When a table is too wide or complex for a typical e-reader screen, restructure it into multiple smaller tables or vertically organized sections.**

For example, instead of:

| Principle | Explanation         | Advantages          | Disadvantages       | Practical Application |
|:----------|:--------------------|:--------------------|:--------------------|:----------------------|
| Example   | Long explanation... | Long explanation... | Long explanation... | Long explanation...   |

Prefer separate, smaller structures:

**Principle: Example**

| Attribute             | Description |
|:----------------------|:------------|
| Explanation           | ...         |
| Advantages            | ...         |
| Disadvantages         | ...         |
| Practical application | ...         |

Alternatively, use a clearly structured list if that provides a better reading experience.

Do not simply split tables arbitrarily. Preserve the logical relationship between the original columns and their data.


<br/><br/>






### 3. Images, Graphs, and Diagrams

All images and visual elements must be suitable for display on e-reader screens.

Requirements:

- Preserve meaningful original images, diagrams, graphs, and illustrations.
- Scale images proportionally.
- Never stretch or distort images.
- Ensure that images do not exceed the available reading area.
- Avoid unnecessarily large images that increase EPUB file size.
- Preserve image quality sufficiently for labels, annotations, and details to remain legible.
- Place images close to the relevant explanatory text.
- Preserve captions and explanatory descriptions.
- Avoid relying exclusively on color to communicate information.

**Wide or complex visual elements must be adapted for narrow screens.**

Where appropriate:

- Split wide diagrams into multiple logically connected segments.
- Divide complex graphs into smaller visual sections.
- Separate multi-part illustrations into individual images.
- Split oversized charts into multiple smaller charts while preserving their relationships.
- Reorganize visual information vertically when horizontal presentation would be difficult to read.

When splitting a visual:
- Preserve the original information.
- Maintain the correct sequence.
- Use clear labels and captions.
- Indicate when multiple images represent parts of the same original diagram.
- Avoid splitting elements in a way that makes them difficult to understand independently.

Do not unnecessarily split simple images or diagrams that already fit typical e-reader screens.


<br/><br/>






### 4. Graphs and Complex Visualizations

Graphs and charts should remain understandable on small screens.

- Avoid excessively wide graphs.
- Prefer vertically oriented charts where appropriate.
- Keep labels readable.
- Avoid overcrowding.
