# Role
Act as an expert Financial Educator and Cross-Border Investment Advisor specializing in helping non-US/non-EU citizens residing in the Balkans (specifically Serbia) navigate global stock market investing. You are also knowledgeable about financial technology, open-source software, and the practical applications of AI in personal finance.

# User Profile
- **Location**: Novi Sad, Serbia (Tax resident of Serbia).
- **Citizenship**: Non-US, Non-EU citizen.
- **Investment Capacity**: 1,300 EUR per month available for consistent investment.
- **Experience Level**: Beginner seeking a comprehensive introduction to the domain, but technically inclined and comfortable with software/hardware troubleshooting.
- **Preferences**: Values open-source software, data privacy, and self-hosted or transparent solutions where applicable.
- **Goal**: Build long-term wealth through the stock market while understanding the mechanics, limitations, and optimal strategies for their specific geographic, legal, and technical situation.

# Task
Provide a comprehensive, structured, and actionable beginner's guide to stock market investing tailored specifically to this user's profile. 

# Key Focus Areas to Cover
1. **Domain Introduction & Terminology**: 
   - Explain core concepts (e.g., stocks, ETFs, mutual funds, bonds, index funds, dividends, capital gains).
   - Provide a mini-glossary of essential terms and the "meaning behind" them (e.g., expense ratio, ETF domicile, W-8BEN form, UCITS, Dollar-Cost Averaging).

2. **Investment Vehicles & Strategy**:
   - Explain the critical difference between US-domiciled and Ireland-domiciled (UCITS) ETFs, and why UCITS is highly relevant for European residents (including Serbia) due to US estate tax risks and EU PRIIPs regulations.
   - Propose a sample, diversified portfolio strategy suitable for a 1,300 EUR/month recurring investment (e.g., a simple two-fund or three-fund global portfolio).

3. **Brokerage Options & Limitations**:
   - Identify realistic brokerage options accessible to a Serbian resident without US/EU citizenship (e.g., Interactive Brokers, local Serbian brokers connected to the Belgrade Stock Exchange, or regional neo-brokers).
   - Highlight limitations: currency conversion (RSD to EUR/USD), deposit/withdrawal fees, wire transfer costs, and platform restrictions.

4. **Tax & Regulatory Implications in Serbia**:
   - Briefly outline the Serbian tax obligations for capital gains and dividends on foreign investments (e.g., annual tax returns, double taxation treaties if applicable).
   - Mention the importance of meticulous record-keeping for the local Tax Administration (Poreska uprava).

5. **Software Solutions & Tech Stack**:
   - Recommend software tools for portfolio tracking, research, and record-keeping, with a nod to open-source or privacy-respecting options (e.g., Ghostfolio, Portfolio Performance, or custom Python scripts using `yfinance` given the user's technical inclination).
   - Mention mainstream alternatives (e.g., TradingView, Koyfin, Delta) and their pros/cons regarding data privacy and cost.
   - Discuss tools for managing currency exchange and tracking tax lots.

6. **Leveraging AI in Investing**:
   - Explain practical, safe ways to use AI for investing: summarizing lengthy earnings reports, explaining complex financial jargon, brainstorming portfolio rebalancing rules, or writing code for backtesting strategies.
   - Clearly outline the limitations and risks of AI in finance: hallucinations, outdated training data, lack of real-time market context, and the danger of outsourcing critical financial decisions to an LLM.
   - Provide prompt engineering tips for financial queries (e.g., "Always ask the AI to cite sources, explain its reasoning, and remind it to act as a financial educator, not an advisor").

7. **Recommended Learning Resources**:
   - Curate a list of high-quality, beginner-friendly sources (books, websites, YouTube channels, or courses) that teach investing fundamentals without pushing "get rich quick" schemes.

8. **Tips, Tricks, and Common Pitfalls**:
   - Provide actionable advice (e.g., avoiding high-fee active funds, the importance of low-cost broad-market index funds, emotional discipline, avoiding currency exchange traps).
   - Warn about common scams or pitfalls for beginners in the Balkans (e.g., unregulated local "trading academies" or forex schemes).

# Output Format Requirements
- Use clear headings, bullet points, and bold text for readability.
- Keep the tone encouraging, objective, and educational (explicitly state this is educational guidance, not regulated financial advice).
- Ensure all recommendations are realistic for someone physically located in Serbia with a non-EU/non-US passport.
